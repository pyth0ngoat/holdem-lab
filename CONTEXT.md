## 1. What this project is

**Hold'em Lab** is an applied-probability project (MSc Applied Probability Distributions) on Texas Hold'em:

> *How does the number of players at the table change which hands win, can a simple formula estimate a hand's chance of winning at an n-player table, and what do real hand histories say about it?*

Deliverables (kept deliberately few):
1. **One Jupyter notebook (`holdem_lab.ipynb`)** that runs end to end and produces every result, table and figure the project claims. Its main numpy steps are written inline (Section 11).
2. **`reports/findings.md`**, auto-generated by the notebook: every single thing tested, with numbers, verdict, figure paths, failures and negative results.
3. **`figures/`**: every figure (PNG) and the data behind it (`figures/data/*.csv`).
4. **A simple validated formula** for the win probability of a starting hand at an n-player table, plus the full empirical table (169 hand classes x n = 2..9 with standard errors).
5. **A real-data section built on two open datasets** (UCI Poker Hand and the PHH hand histories of real online play), Section 9.
6. **EXTRA, not part of the report's claims: a live demo (`demo.py`)** where up to 9 people join by QR code. Demo output (G9) is never cited as a result in the report or slides.

Play chips only. No real money. The project is about probability, not gambling advice.

## 3. Poker as a game, and what this project does and does not study

Poker combines **imperfect information** (you cannot see opponents' cards), **probability management** (estimating odds, variance and risk) and **long-term planning** (blinds, bankroll, many hands, adapting to opponents). This project studies the parts that can be handled rigorously with the course's probability theory, and states clearly what is left out.

## 4. Game models and definitions

**Common:** standard 52-card deck; 2 hole cards per player, 5 community cards (flop 3, turn, river); best 5 of 7 wins; ties split. Seeds fixed.

- **Showdown model (default for equity):** all n players reach showdown; each of the n-1 opponents holds uniformly random hole cards from the remaining deck.
- **P_win** (strictly best), **P_tie**, and **equity share** E = P_win + sum over ties of 1/k. Players are exchangeable, so equity shares sum to 1 and **E[equity share of a random starting hand] = 1/n exactly**.
- **Hand classes:** 169 starting classes (13 pairs, 78 suited, 78 offsuit), combo weights 6, 4, 12 out of 1,326.
- **Return per unit staked** (equal contributions of one unit by every player in the pot): R_n = n * E_n - 1. A hand is profitable to play in this model iff E_n > 1/n (the break-even range).
- **Two hand rankings:** the heads-up ranking (by p2 = heads-up equity) and the multiway ranking (by equity at n = 6 and n = 9). Rankings change with n (see G7).
- **Range model for opponents:** an opponent plays the top q% of hands under a stated ranking (default heads-up ranking, sensitivity under the multiway ranking). Archetype grid: q in {8%, 20%, 33%, 50%, 100%} named Rock, TAG, LAG, Calling-station-like, All-hands. A bot's preflop play rate is not the same as the range it takes to showdown.
- **Limp-or-fold showdown game with blinds (strategies, G8):** each hand, small blind 0.5 and big blind 1 are posted; every other seat chooses to play (put in 1 big blind) or fold according to a strategy defined by a hand set; no raising; if nobody calls the big blind wins the small blind; otherwise the pot is split by showdown among players who played. Blinds are forfeited when folding. This is a deliberately simple long-run model of "range width plus blind cost"; it is not no-limit strategy.

## 6. Research gaps and questions

- **G1 Winning hand vs n.** The iid shortcut F_max = F^n ignores the shared board. Measure the error for n = 2..9, expected winning category, probability of a split pot (discrete order statistic). Units 4, 2.
- **G2 Equity distribution and break-even range.** E[equity] = 1/n exactly; shape of the equity distribution (Beta fit), fraction of hands above 1/n, return per unit staked R_n by hand and n. Units 1, 2.
- **G3 Equity is a martingale.** Equity across streets as conditional expectations, law of total variance by street, Rao-Blackwell variance reduction, accuracy of the rule of 2 and 4. Unit 1 and sampling.
- **G4 Selection bias and opponent tightness.** Equity against opponents who play only the top q% (8, 20, 33, 50, 100) under two rankings; how winning hands and the formula shift; optionally estimate q from real showdowns (PHH). Units 2, 3.
- **G5 Luck, sample size, ruin.** Heavy-tailed per-hand profit, CLT convergence rate, hands needed to separate two strategies, Brownian ruin approximation vs simulation. Units 4, 3.
- **G6 Waiting times.** Hands until pocket aces and other events: Geometric, PGF moments, Poisson/NB counts, streaks. Unit 2 and power series.
- **G7 The formula and rank stability.** A simple formula for P_win and equity share by hand class and n (Section 8); do hand rankings change with n (Spearman and Kendall rank correlation between n = 2 and n = 9; which hand types move), and does the formula need a second feature to capture multiway potential?
- **G8 Strategies, blinds and table selection.** With blinds, how does a tight range fare heads-up vs full ring (closed-form blind-cost break-even); two-way ANOVA of profit per hand on strategy x opponent mix, with an interaction F-test calibrated by permutation ("table selection"). Unit 3.
- **G9 Live human calibration.** Unaided vs aided probability guesses in the demo, paired comparison. Unit 3.

**Scope tiers (what goes in the report).**
- **Tier A (core, main text):** G1, G2, G6, G7 and the real-data section (UCI chi-square plus PHH real play). Hypotheses H1, H2, H6, H7.
- **Tier B (choose at most two, one page each):** G3 streets, G4 opponent tightness (also uses the PHH estimate), G5 luck and sample size, G8 blinds and table selection.

## 8. The simple formula (G7) specification

Inputs: hole-card class (ranks, suited or not, pair) and n; optional tightness q.
Building block: **p2** = heads-up equity of the class against one random hand (a 13x13 chart players can see).
Candidates (the agent fits and compares them; do not assume which wins):
- **M0 naive:** E_n ~ p2^(n-1).
- **M1 normalized power law:** E_n(h) ~ (1/n) * (2*p2(h))^beta_n / kappa_n with kappa_n forcing the combo-weighted mean to 1/n; by-hand version (1/n)*(2*p2)^beta_n; test whether beta_n follows a smooth law such as a + b*(n-2).
- **M2 logit-linear:** logit(E_n) = a_n + b_n * logit(p2).
- **M3 no-chart:** predict p2 from simple features (high rank, low rank, pair, suited, gap) by our own OLS, then apply M1.
- **M4 two-feature:** M1 plus a multiway-potential term (suitedness, connectedness, gap) with n-dependent coefficients; motivated by rank reversal (72o beats 32o heads-up but not from six players on). M1 is monotone in p2 and cannot reorder hands, so rank stability decides whether M4 is needed.
Selection: leave-hand-classes-out cross-validation weighted by combos; mean absolute error, maximum error (percentage points), calibration slope, AIC; prefer the simplest formula within the tolerance in config (default 1.0 percentage point); check the 1/n constraint and monotonicity in n; the formula must reproduce the steep fall of premium pairs (for example aces below 50% at six players) to count as valid. Validate on fresh simulated games with a separate seed. If nothing meets tolerance, report the best with its actual error.
Outputs: `figures/data/equity_table.csv`, the formula card section in findings.md (formula, parameters with SEs, error table, limits, worked by-hand example, three-line phone recipe) and `data/formula_params.json` for the demo.

## 16. Hypotheses summary (pre-registered; report every outcome)

H1 iid-max misestimates the winning hand (size and direction measured). H2 equity shares average exactly 1/n; equity distribution is Beta-like; the fraction of profitable hands changes with n. H3 Rao-Blackwell lowers variance; the rule of 2 and 4 errs for large out counts. H4 tighter opponents lower weak-hand equity and shift the formula. H5 ruin approximation matches simulation; CLT is slow for per-hand profit. H6 a simple formula meets the tolerance. H7 hand rankings change with n (72o vs 32o) so a second feature improves the formula. H8 strategy rankings depend on opponent mix (interaction); tight ranges bleed blinds heads-up. H9 players are better calibrated with the aid.
